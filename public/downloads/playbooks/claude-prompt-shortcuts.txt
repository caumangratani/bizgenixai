73 Claude prompt shortcuts (and the exact instruction behind each)
Bizgenix AI playbook · checked 2026-11-28 · https://bizgenix.ai/playbooks/claude-prompt-shortcuts

Read this first
Claude has no built-in "secret codes" or hidden modes. Every shortcut below is a short label for a concrete instruction. Typing `/redteam` or `INVERT` alone does nothing unless Claude already has the instruction in your Claude Project instructions or in the same message.

How to make all 73 shortcuts work:
1. Paste one instruction directly into your prompt whenever you need that behaviour.
2. Save the list in a Claude Project (Project Instructions) with the opening rule: *"When I start or end a message with any shortcut below, apply its exact instruction to my request."*
3. Verify facts yourself. Shortcuts change how Claude analyses or writes—they do not verify numbers, legal rules or product claims.


Copy-ready Project setup line
When I include one or more shortcuts below (such as /redteam or INVERT) in a message, follow the exact instruction written next to each shortcut. Never invent statistics, client names or dates; mark missing facts clearly.


Core Writing & Style
- /ghost — Rewrite this draft in plain, direct prose: use short sentences, active voice and concrete words, cut filler and corporate clichés, and preserve all facts untouched for final human editing.
- /mirror — Study the writing samples I paste here—sentence length, vocabulary, punctuation and structure—and rewrite my draft to match that exact style without inventing new facts.
- /punch — Make every sentence shorter and more direct: put the main verb early, cut adjectives and qualifiers that add no meaning, and remove throat-clearing openings.
- /hook — Give me five distinct opening lines for this piece that earn attention with a specific fact, question or tension—no clickbait and no generic summaries.
- /trim — Cut this text by at least 30% without losing a single fact, number or action item; flag any sentence you kept only for transition.
- /polish — Tighten grammar, flow and clarity so the draft reads professionally to a client or executive while keeping the exact meaning and tone intact.
- /flow — Reorder paragraphs and add clean transitions so each idea leads naturally to the next from opening answer to closing action.
- /rephrase — Rewrite this passage in three structurally different ways—one executive summary, one conversational note, and one bulleted brief—keeping every fact identical.
- /voice — Lock in the tone, reading level and phrasing rules I specify above and keep them consistent across every reply in this conversation.

Artifacts & Creation
- ARTIFACTS — Build a self-contained, working interactive file (HTML/React/SVG/document) I can test immediately, and list any assumptions or sample data clearly.
- /buildme — Design and build a small working tool from this description: keep dependencies minimal, label sample data clearly, and list how to run and verify it.
- DASHBOARD — Create an interactive summary view for the metrics I provide: highlight exceptions first, show period comparisons, and keep calculations transparent.
- PROTOTYPE — Build the smallest testable version of this workflow in one pass so I can try the user flow on sample records before scoping a full build.
- BLUEPRINT — Map out the architecture, file layout, data flow, edge cases and acceptance tests for this project before writing any implementation code.
- WIREFRAME — Lay out the screen hierarchy, sections, copy slots and primary user action in clean text/HTML structure without spending effort on decorative styling.
- GENERATOR — Build a reusable template or script that accepts my structured inputs and outputs consistent, ready-to-review deliverables on demand.

Thinking & Reasoning
- OODA — Analyse this situation in four explicit stages—Observe (raw facts only), Orient (constraints and context), Decide (trade-offs and choice), and Act (first concrete step).
- /deepthink — Break the problem into assumptions, constraints, second-order effects and failure modes step by step before recommending a direction.
- L99 — Skip surface-level generalities: give me an operator-level analysis with concrete constraints, edge cases, exact trade-offs and measurable checks.
- CHAINLOGIC — Show each step of your reasoning from starting facts to conclusion so I can inspect and challenge any link that looks weak.
- /blindspots — List five important questions, risks or missing data points I have not mentioned yet that could invalidate this plan.
- OVERTHINK — Stress-test every detail of this decision: examine edge cases, timing clashes, hidden costs and human-handoff failures before we commit.
- /unpack — Deconstruct this complex topic into its moving parts, explain how each part works in plain words, and show how they depend on one another.
- INVERT — Instead of asking how to succeed, list every reliable way this project could fail or stall, then design rules to prevent each one.
- XRAY — Strip away buzzwords and framing to state what is actually happening underneath: incentives, bottlenecks, real numbers and who decides.

Learning & Mastery
- /teachme — Teach me this concept step by step as a practitioner: explain why it matters, give a concrete business example, and ask me one check question before moving on.
- GAPFINDER — Assess what I already know from my summary below, pinpoint the exact concepts or skills I am missing, and order them from highest impact to lowest.
- /eli5 — Explain this simply using everyday words and a familiar Indian business analogy—zero jargon, short sentences, clear takeaway.
- MASTERCLASS — Teach this subject at practitioner depth: mental models, real trade-offs, common mistakes made by teams, and how experts verify quality.
- SPEEDRUN — Give me the 20% of concepts and steps I need to understand and apply in the next 30 minutes to get a working result safely.
- /mentor — Advise me like an experienced operator who knows my constraints: tell me directly what to prioritise, what to drop, and what to ask next.
- CRASHCOURSE — Give me a structured five-minute briefing: core definition, why it matters, three worked examples, and three cautions.

Analysis & Strategy
- /redteam — Attack this plan aggressively from the perspective of a sceptical buyer, competitor and auditor; list every weak claim, cost risk and execution flaw.
- PARETO — Identify the few inputs, customers, products or tasks in this list that account for most of the result, and say what can safely wait.
- WARGAME — Simulate how competitors, customers and regulators are likely to react over the next two quarters if we make this move.
- /premortem — Imagine it is six months from now and this initiative failed completely: write the most probable reasons it failed and the early warning signs we ignored.
- LEVERAGE — Find the single bottleneck or change in this workflow where one unit of effort improves multiple downstream outcomes.
- /audit — Review this work against clear acceptance criteria: list factual gaps, unclear ownership, formatting errors and unverified claims.
- BOTTLENECK — Trace this process from input to cash or completion and identify the exact step where work piles up, waits or fails most often.
- /scenario — Model three scenarios from these inputs—conservative, base and downside—showing the assumptions, cash/time impact and trigger to switch plans.

Creative & Content
- /viral — Rewrite this core idea with three sharper angles built around a concrete mistake, number or counter-intuitive truth from real work—no clickbait.
- HOOKS10 — Write ten distinct opening hooks for this topic—using questions, contrast, specific numbers and real situations—and label who each hook fits.
- /storysell — Turn this dry feature or lesson into a short real-world narrative: situation, friction, decision, what changed, and one takeaway for the reader.
- REMIX — Combine two unrelated frameworks or examples from my notes into one fresh, practical post outline with a clear point of view.
- /angles — Give me ten genuinely different content angles on this single topic—for owners, finance heads, operations teams and sceptics.
- CLIFFHANGER — Write three closing lines that invite thoughtful discussion or point naturally to the next step without sounding forced or salesy.
- /captionme — Write three concise social captions for this post or video: put the searchable topic phrase in line one, keep the body crisp, and end with one clear action.
- POLARIZE — State a clear, defensible point of view on this industry habit—explain what most people do wrong, why it hurts, and what to do instead.
- /banger — Condense this insight into one memorable, plain-spoken sentence worth quoting—no clichés and no exaggerated claims.

Coding & Technical
- /debug — Trace this bug systematically: list the most likely root causes from the error and code shown, how to verify each in one command, and the minimal fix.
- REFACTOR — Simplify this working code without changing behaviour: remove duplication, split mixed responsibilities, and keep interfaces backward-compatible.
- /shipit — Review this code as a pre-release check: identify missing validation, error handling, edge cases, secrets exposure and tests to run before deploying.
- ARCHITECT — Propose a clean system design for these requirements: components, data schema, boundaries, failure recovery and what not to build yet.
- AUTOMATE — Turn this manual multi-step task into a repeatable script or workflow with clear inputs, dry-run checks, logs and a stop/rollback rule.
- /optimize — Find the real performance or payload bottleneck first using measurements, then suggest targeted changes with expected impact and trade-offs.

Research & Deep Dives
- /deepdive — Investigate this topic thoroughly across mechanisms, pricing, constraints, failure modes and alternatives; separate verified facts from open questions.
- FACTCHECK — Inspect every claim, statistic and quote in this draft: mark what is verified from provided sources, what is unverified, and what needs a dated citation.
- /investigate — Examine this problem like an analyst: list what evidence we already have, what is contradictory, and the exact primary checks needed to settle it.
- TRENDSCAN — Summarise what has actually changed in this market or toolset recently based on verifiable releases and buyer behaviour—skip hype.
- /digest — Distil this long document into a one-page executive brief: core thesis, key numbers with dates, decisions required and open risks.
- DOSSIER — Compile a structured briefing on this company, product or topic using only verified facts: overview, positioning, strengths, gaps and sources.

Power Commands
- /godmode — Use full structured reasoning for this task: state assumptions, explore alternatives, check numbers and edge cases, and deliver an execution-ready output.
- /autoprompt — Turn my rough goal below into a clear, structured prompt with Role, Context, Task, Constraints, Input placeholders and Output Format.
- MEGAPROMPT — Build a comprehensive reusable prompt template for this recurring workflow, including quality gates, edge-case handling and review steps.
- PERSONA — Adopt the specific practitioner role I name—such as a chartered accountant, operations head or B2B buyer—and evaluate this strictly from their seat.
- /memory — Summarise the confirmed rules, facts, decisions and preferences established in this conversation into a clean context block I can paste into future chats.
- PROMPTFIX — Diagnose why my prompt below gave weak output and rewrite it with clear criteria, constraints and concrete examples.

Direct & Decision Modes
- /nofilter — Skip politeness and padding: tell me plainly what is weak in this idea, what assumptions look unrealistic, and what you would change first.
- BEASTMODE — Hold the output to the highest practical standard: verify logic, tighten every section, flag weak evidence and leave zero placeholders unless marked.
- CEOMODE — Evaluate this strictly as a business owner accountable for cash, time and reputation: focus on ROI, risk, team bandwidth and this week’s decision.
- /negotiate — Prepare a structured negotiation brief: our walk-away line, the other side’s likely priorities, concessions to trade, and exact responses to three objections.
- FOUNDER — Think like an owner-operator building with a lean team: favour fast proof on real customers, low fixed overhead and repeatable systems.
- SENTINEL — Act as final quality control before this ships: scan for factual errors, unapproved claims, missing links, broken edge cases and unclear instructions.

Shortcuts that work well together
- Before launching a service or offer: `/unpack` + `/redteam` + `/premortem`
- Before publishing an article or client draft: `/audit` + `FACTCHECK` + `/trim` + `SENTINEL`
- Weekly business review: `BOTTLENECK` + `PARETO` + `CEOMODE`
