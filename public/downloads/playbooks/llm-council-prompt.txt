The LLM Council prompt: five expert views before one answer
Bizgenix AI playbook · checked 2026-11-28 · https://bizgenix.ai/playbooks/llm-council-prompt

The idea
Most people ask an AI assistant one question and take one answer. A single answer misses risks and better options. This prompt makes the assistant argue with itself first: five advisors look at your problem from five angles, then a chairman weighs them and gives one recommendation.


The five advisors
- The Contrarian looks for flaws: risks, weak assumptions and ways the plan fails.
- The First Principles Thinker ignores industry habits and rebuilds the answer from basics.
- The Expansionist looks for the bigger opportunity and points of leverage.
- The Outsider knows nothing about your industry and questions what insiders take for granted.
- The Executor cares only about what to do next: priorities, resources and an action plan.


The chairman
After the five have spoken, the chairman reviews every view, lists where they agree and disagree, settles the conflicts and gives a final recommendation with key insights, major risks, biggest opportunities and clear next actions.


The prompt
Act as an LLM Council.
I want five advisors to analyze my problem before providing a final answer.
Advisor 1: Contrarian
Find flaws, risks, weak assumptions and reasons this may fail.
Advisor 2: First Principles Thinker
Ignore conventional wisdom and rebuild the solution from first principles.
Advisor 3: Expansionist
Identify larger opportunities, leverage points and upside scenarios.
Advisor 4: Outsider
Analyze the problem as someone completely unfamiliar with the industry.
Advisor 5: Executor
Create a practical action plan focused on implementation.
After all advisors finish:
Act as Chairman.
Review every viewpoint.
Identify agreements and disagreements.
Resolve conflicts.
Provide a final recommendation.
Finish with:
- Top 3 Risks
- Top 3 Opportunities
- Next 7 Days Action Plan
- Final Verdict
My problem is:
[INSERT PROBLEM HERE]


Make them debate
Add this line at the end:

Challenge each other's reasoning before the Chairman provides the final verdict.

The advisors then argue with each other instead of giving five separate answers, which usually gives a deeper and more balanced result.


Questions it suits
- A business decision: should we open a second branch this year?
- Marketing: how should we win our first ten clients for a new service?
- Hiring: should we hire a manager or promote from inside?
- Product: should we build this feature or buy a tool?

It is a thinking aid. The decision, and the facts you feed it, stay with you.
